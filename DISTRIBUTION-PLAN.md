# Distribution plan: Perimetrr beta

## Deal Snapshot

- Product: browser-based workplace attendance, Perimeter checks, Command Center, hybrid schedules and organization-scoped Watch Tower.
- Account/opportunity: no named customer or existing deal. First-sale motion from user-supplied product context, not CRM-backed pipeline.
- Stage: pre-launch beta. Close date: unknown. Time window: first four weeks after live acceptance tests pass.
- Coverage: conversation and repository evidence; no customer interviews, conversion history or approved paid billing. Targets below are experiments, not forecasts.

## Deal Map

Start with owner-managed companies with one location and a small roster, or hybrid teams with an identifiable attendance problem. This is an initial buyer hypothesis, not validated market research. Do not start with hundreds of branches: support load, enrollment controls and procurement expectations are higher.

Offer: a guided fictional-data demo, followed by a limited pilot only after identity, privacy and retention safeguards are agreed. Position as a simpler attendance workflow—not proof against fraud or a payroll replacement. Keep Free up to five staff as product policy, not an excuse to harvest employee data.

Sequence: demonstrate → discover their current workflow → agree a small test → help activate → collect permissioned feedback → decide whether to continue. Do not sell unimplemented billing or enterprise guarantees.

## Procurement Risk Register

| Risk | Severity | Suggested owner | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sensitive real employee data before safeguards | High | Founder/customer | Fictional demo first; agree DPA, notice and retention before real data |
| First-claim identity loophole | High | Founder | Admin-supervised enrollment; resolve before broad rollout |
| No functioning email/support or recovery | High | Founder | Live delivery and recovery acceptance tests before invitations |
| Unvalidated need and limited support capacity | Medium | Founder | Five guided pilots, not a mass launch |
| Paid offer does not match working checkout | High | Founder | Beta first; no payment collection until billing verified |

## Prioritized Next Actions

1. **Week 1 — founder:** Invite five known business owners/HR contacts individually to a 15-minute fictional-data walkthrough. Ask what they use today, what fails, and who owns the buying decision. No scraped list or bulk unsolicited messages. Outcome: five grounded conversations.
2. **Week 1 — founder:** Publish a captioned 45–60-second walkthrough using fictional names. Show staff check-in, the admin report and one honest limitation. Use LinkedIn and existing WhatsApp business communities only where promotion is permitted. Outcome: clear demonstration, not invented social proof.
3. **Week 2 — founder:** Recruit up to five guided pilots. Help each create a workspace, configure a Perimeter and complete their first verified check-in/report. Start with fictional data; use real records only after the compliance gates. Suggested pilot length: 14 days, adjustable after discussion.
4. **Week 2–3 — founder:** Publish two useful pieces: an attendance-sheet workflow comparison and a practical hybrid-work scheduling guide. Link to the demo. Claims must be reproducible; no unverifiable savings or competitor attacks. Outcome: relevant inbound interest.
5. **Week 3 — founder:** Request an introduction from satisfied testers and approach a small number of HR consultants/payroll implementers through existing relationships. Ask permission before quoting reviews, showing logos or publishing results. Label any incentives.
6. **Week 4 — founder:** Review anonymous aggregate counts manually: invited organizations, demos, activated workspaces, repeat weekly use, pairing failures and support time. No employee location/device credentials in marketing analytics. Interview inactive teams. Outcome: decide whether onboarding or positioning needs repair.
7. **After repeat use is demonstrated — founder:** Optional paid experiment, not launch prerequisite: choose one channel and cap the first test at a founder-approved budget (suggested US$50–100 equivalent). Use one buyer-specific message and the working demo. No retargeting pixels until consent requirements are handled. Stop if leads do not activate; clicks alone are not success.

Suggested go/no-go evidence before paid acquisition: at least three independently active pilot organizations in two consecutive weeks, acceptable support load, and no unresolved high-severity access/recovery defects. These are proposed thresholds, not industry benchmarks.

Draft outreach: “I’m building Perimetrr to make workplace attendance and hybrid schedules easier to manage. Could I show you a 15-minute demo with fictional data and hear how your team handles attendance today? No employee data or payment needed.” Sending is not authorized by this document.

## Evidence Gaps

No validated target segment, willing-to-pay evidence, reliable channel conversion rates, ad budget, customer commitments or testimonials exist in the supplied evidence. Close those gaps through conversations and small pilots rather than buying broad traffic. Commercial and privacy readiness remain gates for real-data pilots.

## Inference Notes

Owner-managed small teams are suggested because they offer a shorter feedback loop than large enterprise procurement. This is a hypothesis to validate, not a claim that large companies cannot use Perimetrr. Next refinement: turn the first five pilot conversations into a simple activation and feedback tracker.
